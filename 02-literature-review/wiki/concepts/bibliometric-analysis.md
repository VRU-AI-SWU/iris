## Definition
**Bibliometric analysis** studies a research field quantitatively through its
publications: co-citation and co-word networks, keyword clustering, publication and
citation trends over time. It maps what a field has studied and where its attention has
moved, rather than contributing a method to it.

Typical use is to establish that a research area is active, fragmented, or under-served —
framing evidence for a new contribution.

## Relevance to Iris
Contributes **framing, not method**. [[aljohani-2022-curriculum-skill-gap-bibliometric]]
establishes that curriculum↔market skill-gap analysis is a recognised, active research area
— useful for a paper's introduction, and the reason its relevance is recorded as *medium*
rather than *high*.

It is worth noting what a bibliometric survey of this field would and would not have
surfaced, because the project has direct experience of the limit. A publication-based map
of the field cannot show that a government has published a national skill standard, since
that standard has no publication. The lesson recorded in
[`instruction.md`](../../.claude/instruction.md) after
[[q-thai-ontology]] reversed — that infrastructure questions require searching ministry
and open-data sources directly — is the same limitation seen from the practitioner side.
